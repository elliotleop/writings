# The Indemnity Ladder: How Baskets, Caps and Price Move Together in Acquisition Agreements

**Elliot Past and Rory Campbell** · 2026 · Working paper

[Read the PDF](The_Indemnity_Ladder.pdf)

Private acquisition agreements limit a seller's liability for breached representations with a ladder of negotiated terms: a per-claim de minimis, a basket that losses must exceed before the seller pays, a cap on total exposure, and a survival period. This paper asks whether those terms move together. Comparing deals of the same price, does a higher cap go with a higher basket?

The ladder is extracted from tens of thousands of agreements filed with the SEC. The extraction is checked against 279 hand-marked agreements, and hypotheses registered in advance are tested on three held-out samples spanning 1995–2025.

**Findings**

- The basket rises with both the price and the cap, each holding the other fixed. Which one it follows depends on the industry.
- **Manufacturing and services** (383 held-out deals): the basket follows the price. Elasticities are 0.60 on price and 0.33 [0.20, 0.45] on cap.
- **Financial sector**, including REITs and insurers (81 deals): the basket follows the cap. Elasticities are 0.77 [0.63, 0.90] on cap and 0.23 on price.
- A single pooled elasticity, 0.51 [0.43, 0.58] on 673 deals, mixes the two regimes.
- Deals with representations-and-warranties insurance have far lower caps and somewhat lower baskets.

The estimates cover deals whose basket and cap are both stated in dollars, about two-thirds of those in which both terms are found. The paper's model-use statement describes how generative AI was used.
