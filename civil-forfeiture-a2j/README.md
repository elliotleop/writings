# Civil Forfeiture and the Access to Justice Gap

**Elliot Past** · 2026 · Access-to-justice project paper, Chicago-Kent College of Law

[Read the PDF](Civil_Forfeiture_and_the_Access_to_Justice_Gap.pdf)

Illinois law enforcement collected $1.2 billion through civil forfeiture from 2000 to 2023. The median forfeiture from 2019 to 2023 was $1,031 in currency and $7,050 for a car. Those amounts are too small to justify hiring counsel, and two-thirds of Cook County property owners facing forfeiture proceed pro se.

The paper describes Illinois's two forfeiture channels, the Drug Asset Forfeiture Procedure Act and the Criminal Code, and the 2017 reforms. It draws on weekly observation of forfeiture court. It focuses on two pitfalls for self-represented owners: the fast civil timeline faced by people who are also defending criminal charges, and the innocent-owner hearing, whose seven-element affidavit is hard to prepare without legal training.

The project built guided interviews to help self-represented owners with the forfeiture forms, including a hardship motion that can temporarily return a seized vehicle. The paper documents that design.
