# Where Should We Draw the Line? Pro Se AI Use, Privilege, and the Laboratory of Justice

**Elliot Past** · 2026 · Student note, Chicago-Kent College of Law

[Read the PDF](Where_Should_We_Draw_the_Line.pdf)

In the first half of 2026, five courts ruled on whether a litigant's conversations with a generative-AI chatbot are protected from discovery: *United States v. Heppner* (S.D.N.Y.), *Warner v. Gilbarco* (E.D. Mich.), *Morgan v. V2X* (D. Colo.), *Tate Group Automotive v. Legacy Automotive Capital* (Tex. Bus. Ct.), and *Assini v. Hayward* (N.Y. Sup. Ct.). This note reads them together. What began as a split became a conversation, with each court quoting, distinguishing and refining the ones before it.

**Thesis.** Protection should turn on whether the litigant already has a sphere of protection. *Heppner* is right on its narrow facts: a counseled, well-resourced defendant had no need to consult a consumer chatbot. A pro se litigant has no such sphere, and for them the AI conversation is the work of preparing for litigation, so it should be protected work product under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 26(b)(3).
